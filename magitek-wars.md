## 1. Overview
- **An original 16-bit-style card battler of steam and magic, not a port, built on a copy of the Annals of Scrivenearth engine and re-skinned, with a campaign roguelike on top.** Owner (2026-11-26): "Original 16-bit direction is fine ... public repo, lets call it Magitek Wars." The world (an empire running on gloamsteam drawn from glass roots under a dying continent), names, cards, sprites and art are written and drawn fresh; nothing from any published game, book or show (PLAN D1). "Magitek" is the title only; in the world the machines are **gloamwork**, so in-world text never leans on another game's term (D8). *(updated 2026-11-28)* Owner: "I don't know what the hell gloam is, it sounds dumb ... what the hell is a boiler? Let each one of the nations defend a crystal, instead of a boiler." Since D24 the substance is **crystal**, the machines are **crystalwork**, and the engine's Spire is printed as the **Crystal**. Where this entry says gloam or Boiler, read crystal and Crystal.
- Shape: Annals' rules unchanged (1 human against an AI, two Lines of six, heroes that Ascend, a 20-point Spire printed as the **Boiler**, glyphs printed as **tactics**; [annals-of-scrivenearth.md](annals-of-scrivenearth.md)), 300 cards and 24 heroes in six factions, 24 premade decks plus one single-faction deck per faction, three AI levels (Recruit, Veteran, Marshal). Modes: QUICK BATTLE and CAMPAIGN (spec 20: three acts of seven floors with skirmishes, elites, rest stops, supply depots and a boss, one growing deck and a Boiler that carries its damage between battles).
- Owner decisions (do not re-ask): copy the Annals engine ("Copy Annals engine", D4); a style LoRA on FLUX.2 klein ("Flux.2 KLEIN is fine.", D3); the owner's reference sheet is the character style ("here's the art sytle i want you to use", D11, measured in `docs/ART-STYLE.md` section 4a: about 2.8 head-heights, head about 35% of height, 1 px dark outline, 3-4 tones lit from the top left); the owner's own Gemini sprite sheets are training data, minus three figures that copy a published series (D3 amended); "Train v2" (D3); "Do all of it. Get the game working into a point where I can play test all the decks." (D16); "Then start working on the roguelike." (D18); licence all rights reserved (D19). Still open and built on as they are (owner: "Keep, I'll revise later"): the interface concept "field dispatches from a war of steam and gloam, on a 16-bit cartridge" (D5, Q2) and the faction re-skin map (D12, Q5).

## 4. Decisions worth reusing
- **Copy a finished engine into a new game instead of linking it** (D4): the copy brings its whole gate (rule tests, the text-versus-data audit, the mutation pass, the AI bench), so the new game starts green and the re-skin is checked by tools that already exist. Change every storage key and the save's game id at copy time when both games deploy to the same `github.io` origin.
- **Keep the numbers and rules, replace the words** (`docs/RESKIN.md`): Spire printed as Boiler, glyph as tactic, cast as play; faction, card and hero names changed; every keyword, trigger word and number kept; card ids, faction ids and the `tribe` field (filters read it) kept. The audit gained a pair check that any card touching a Spire selector says "Boiler" (`tools/audit.ts` line 86).
- **Two screen sizes on one canvas** (D17): the title and gallery at the console's 256 x 224 doubled, the battle at the hi-res 512 x 448, because two Lines of six, a hand of ten, the stack, the log and readable card text did not fit 256 x 224. Same font and palette at both sizes.
- **Rendered art keeps its own reduced palette** (D14): code-drawn interface pieces stay on the 31-colour house palette, while LoRA renders are snapped with up to 96 colours a scene and 32 a figure. Forcing renders onto the 31 colours flattened them.
- **A campaign run needs one engine hook only** (R-X3, `Setup.startSpire`): the run writes the player's Boiler back after each battle and passes it in for the next. Foe deck size, foe Boiler and AI level rise by act and node kind from a table in spec 20 (R-R6).
- **The LoRA dataset rules** (D3, `docs/LORA.md` section 3): only our own renders, the owner's own generations, or CC0 / permissive art with the licence recorded; never ripped game art or "in the style of" a game or artist; figures that copy a published series are cut before training; a source ledger with each sheet's sha256 (`lora/SOURCES.csv`).

## 5. Lessons for the series
- *(added 2026-11-27)* **A FLUX.2 klein 4B style LoRA trained on the 4B Base works on the distilled klein in ComfyUI.** ai-toolkit config (`lora/mkw16_v1.yaml`, `mkw16_v2.yaml`): `arch: flux2_klein_4b`, `black-forest-labs/FLUX.2-klein-base-4B`, LoRA rank 16 / alpha 16, AdamW 8-bit, lr 1e-4, batch 1, buckets 512/768, horizontal flip, fp8 (`qfloat8`) base and text encoder, low-VRAM mode, EMA 0.99, caption dropout 0.05, trigger `mkw16`. On the RTX 5080 (16 GB): about 1 s a step; v1's 2000 steps in 32 minutes plus about 3 minutes of sampling per 250 steps; v2's 3000 steps in 50 minutes; a first download of about 16 GB (transformer 7.75 GB, Qwen3-4B 8.1 GB). Use: `LoraLoaderModelOnly` at strength 1.0 on `flux-2-klein-4b`, 4 steps, cfg 1, `ConditioningZeroOut` as the negative (`tools/art/render.mjs`). Training samples on Base (28 steps) moved toward the sheets by step 250 and matched outline, saturation and proportions by step 1500 (`lora/RUNS.md`). Flux Studio had no LoRA field at `433dd50` (`docs/LORA.md` section 7; D19 lists one for the overnight run), so the renders went to ComfyUI directly.
- *(added 2026-11-27)* **A LoRA trained only on standing figures turns other subjects into figures; render v2's data with v1.** v1 (51 sprites, all standing people on navy) drew "a brass clockwork owl on a lamp post" as a man holding a lamp post. v2 added 64 prompts (scenes, buildings, machines, creatures, items, people in motion) at three seeds each, rendered with v1 at 0.9 (192 renders in about 15 minutes), 121 picked on labelled contact sheets and captioned with the kind of image ("a 16-bit game background scene, wide view, no characters", "... item icon, centred ..."). v2 put the owl on the lamp post and drew the boiler machine as a boiler on legs; people stayed as good as v1; scenes and items were already good with v1 once the caption named the kind of image. Still wrong in v2: a second prop when a hand is free (two wrenches, a lantern plus a loose flame), a paddle boat with no paddle wheel, letter-like marks on a compass rim. The owner's verdict on v2 is not recorded (`lora/RUNS.md`: "pending").
- *(added 2026-11-27)* **Snapping training data to a pixel grid did not make the LoRA's pixels crisper.** v2 trained on renders snapped to a true 3 px grid, and its output was no crisper than v1's under magnification. Every render still goes through the snap step before it ships (`lora/RUNS.md`).
- *(added 2026-11-27)* **Snap renders to a fixed grid, one colour per cell by vote.** The renders' pseudo-pixels do not sit on a steady pitch, so a detected pitch is unreliable; the training data was snapped at a forced 3 px (`tools/lora/clean.mjs` keeps a pitch finder but takes a forced pitch), and game art is divided into exactly its logical size (a scene to 256 x 224 or 64 x 48, `tools/art/snap.mjs`). Each cell takes the most common colour of its inner pixels (5-bit buckets, a 1 px margin skipped when the cell is 4 px or wider), then the image is cut to N colours with no dithering. Figures are cut from their flat ground by a flood fill from the border, reduced to a logical height and given hard 1-bit transparency.
- *(added 2026-11-27)* **Stray interface lettering is the reason the pick notes name most often.** Card art was two seeds a card on the prefix `mkw16 pixel art, a 16-bit game cutscene, close view, full frame,` plus a plain subject. Of 332 faces, 21 shipped their second seed (stray glyphs in a corner, interface lettering, or a doubled prop), 12 needed three more seeds for lettering or the wrong subject (10 then passed), and 2 carried lettering on all five seeds and ship as NO ART until a reworded render passes (`tools/art/card-picks.txt`, `card-retry.mjs`). In the v2 dataset, renders with stray lettering, doubled parts or digits were among the 71 of 192 left out (`lora/picks-v2.txt`). The repo does not record what the "cutscene, close view, full frame" wording was compared against, or whether it was chosen to keep frames or borders out of card art; the two failing cards were reworded "as a still moment" for the next run (`tools/art/jobs-night.mjs`). Budget extra seeds, and read every candidate's corners on the contact sheet.
- *(added 2026-11-27)* **Some card subjects drift to a modern-war look under a pixel-art LoRA.** Ten cards were picked by eye from the contact sheets as drifted; their re-render adds "Steam age, nineteenth-century uniforms and riveted iron, no modern weapons or vehicles." (`tools/art/jobs-night.mjs`; result not yet recorded).
- *(added 2026-11-27)* **Re-skin a card set with one agent per faction, then check it against the data.** One agent per faction wrote each card's and ascended hero's proposed name and art subject (`tools/art/cards/<faction>.mjs`, one entry per card in the engine file's order) against the re-skin map and `docs/WORLD.md` (D15). The printed text follows `docs/RESKIN.md`: change setting words, not sentence shapes, so the text-versus-data audit still parses it; 0 FAIL is the bar. The gate after the re-skin: 472 tests, the audit over 332 faces, the mutation pass.
- *(added 2026-11-27)* **Drive a canvas battle screen entirely from `legalActions`.** Each click narrows the current legal actions, so the screen can never offer a move the engine would refuse and never makes a choice for the player (rules 8 and 12); a click on a card or unit that cannot act shows the engine's own reason (`whyNotPlay`, `whyNotDeclare`) in the prompt line (`src/match/board.ts`).
- *(added 2026-11-27)* **Play-test every deck through the real screen with the AI on both seats.** `?auto` makes the AI play both seats of the battle screen, `&fast` drops the AI delay to 0, and a read-only hook `window.__mkw.battle(mine, foe)` starts any pairing from the setup list (`src/main.ts`, `src/match/board.ts`). All 30 decks played a full battle this way with no error (13-17 by seat, `STATUS.md`). It drives the screen code the human uses, which a headless engine sim does not.
- *(added 2026-11-27)* **Bench a campaign with an AI stand-in before anyone plays it** (`tools/runsim.ts 3 normal`: 18 runs, 3 per faction; BALANCE R-1). The first starter (20 of each faction's cheapest cards) won the first battle 13 of 18 times but no run survived act 1, some dying with Boiler left by drawing out a 20-card deck. A 24-card starter (two copies each of the units with the most power plus health at costs 1-4, plus the lead hero) and foes keeping one hero copy: first battle 18 of 18, 4 of 18 past act 1, 2 reached act 3, none won. No card numbers changed; judge again after the owner's first runs.
- *(added 2026-11-27)* **Raw klein renders were rejected for 16-bit art; a style LoRA was the answer the owner asked for.** See the D13 quotes in section 3. The code-drawn title was "too 8-bit"; the owner wanted a richer look, which is also why rendered art keeps its own palette (D14).

# The owner's standing rules

Earned across more than 30 projects; apply them without re-asking. *(2026-11-03)* Moved out of `README.md` with their numbering unchanged, so "README rule N" in an older doc or game repo means rule N here.

1. **Playable first.** Something the owner can open on Pages and play beats test coverage. Every stage ends in something clickable, with a screenshot.
2. **Fidelity is the product.** Mechanics ported 1:1 from the current edition; never tune printed numbers; log every ambiguity as `RULING: <rule> | <what we did> | <why>` with a status word instead of blocking.
3. **"Mallet posture" on IP.** Real unit/weapon/ability/team names are fine; all prose (rules text, lore, UI copy) in our own words; no publisher art, icons or logos; official PDFs and community data stay in gitignored `docs/sources/`; README and UI say "unofficial, non-commercial fan project". GW iconography in generated figures is an accepted playtest placeholder.
4. **Do the legwork; research before assuming.** Free downloads, $0 checkouts, Vassal modules, fan wikis via curl, product photos: all fair game. Only stop for passwords, payment or account creation. Never ship a stand-in or "unverified" value after one blocked source.
5. **Answer before building.** When the owner asks questions, answer and propose, then wait for the go. Explicit instructions mean act.
6. **Cost-sensitive.** Sonnet implements, the session model integrates and verifies only where rules are dense; one or two agents per milestone with exact file lists; no adversarial review loops by default; about 2.5M tokens per push; check-ins of 10 lines or fewer. Mallet's big-spec experiment (4.8M before anything playable) is not to be repeated.
7. **Art direction is set once per project and not re-litigated.** *(rewritten 2026-11-03)* For miniature figures the house style is Mallet 42k `docs/FIGURE-STYLE.md`, approved by the owner on 2026-10-31 ("Lock it in. Those are way better."): serious and highly detailed like a premium painted miniature, about 2.8 head-heights with the head or helmet about 35% of the height, very broad armoured shoulders about 1.8 times the head's width, short thick legs about one head long, a covered or stern face, and matte realistic paint with edge highlights. Attach `figure-style-reference.jpg` to the first prompt of each army chat and describe the figure in measurements and materials only (rule 23). The five locked test figures are in Mallet 42k `art/unit-concepts/style-tests/`. This replaced the earlier large-head style (about 2-2.5 heads, head about 40% of height, rounded shapes, glossy paint), whose labels pushed the image model to big-eyed, baby-faced figures. Check the image, not the prompt. Concept approval is delegated to Claude; send contact sheets as FYI. Lock the figure look early (a late lock threw away a whole v1 set).
8. **No native tooltips in games**, prompts name what they decide, open information is a rule, never assume a choice, every displayed number comes from the engine.
9. **Audio is judged by ear on `sounds.html`**; say "not verified by ear" until the owner listens. The synth score was rejected once ("the music sucks"), so real generated music is expected. Since 2026-10-17 the local Audio Studio replaces ElevenLabs.
10. **GPU etiquette:** announce GPU work; never two GPU jobs at once; never kill node by image name. The owner waived the ask-first gate on 2026-10-17 for Fell-in and said to use the studios to develop the look and sound.
11. **Privacy:** credit "evilgouki"; never the real name or home path in anything that can become public; grep before every commit.
12. Questions go through the multiple-choice UI, batched (two batches of at most 4), recommended option first, everything else stated as a default.
13. **Pipeline everything; never idle a stage.** Each finished item moves to the next stage straight away: a concept PNG goes into the GLB queue the moment it renders, and a GLB is finalised and wired the moment it lands. Both GPUs (this PC and Alteisen) stay busy while concepts are still coming. The same goes for code: a team's verification starts when its implementer finishes, not when the whole batch does. Waiting for a full batch before starting the next stage was called out as inefficient (Murder Squad, 2026-10-18). **Quality gates also run per item, inside the stream.** Each concept passes a measurable check before it is kept: *(updated 2026-11-03)* a side-by-side with `figure-style-reference.jpg` or the locked test figures, a head of about 35% of figure height and about 2.8 head-heights overall, broad shoulders, and a stern or covered face (rule 7). The coordinator reviews the first image and the contact sheet of each team before that team's GLBs start. "Check each image" with no concrete reference let 32 off-model concepts through, and the owner caught it, not us (2026-10-24).
14. *(added 2026-10-26, Murder Squad PLAN, owner)* **Official sources replace invented content as soon as they exist.** Three invented killzone layouts were deleted and replaced by the six official tournament cards measured on the 1" grid (2026-10-18); buildings are drawn from the rules geometry because generic GLB walls let operatives "walk through" walls (owner bug report 2026-10-22).

21. *(added 2026-10-30, shadowforge, owner)* **Every game gets its own look and feel. A template supplies the structure, never the skin.** "The look and feel is too much like Lorecannae. I wanted it to be unique look and feel for each game ... Xross forge is an excellent example." Shadowforge (Shadowverse: Evolve) copied lorecannae's interface and only re-coloured it: the same centred menu dialog, gold filigree frames, star card backs, serif type, board frame and hand fan ([shadowforge.md](shadowforge.md) has the before and after). Xross Forge started from the same lorecannae scaffold and is the model: one presentation concept drawn from the game's own world (its D7, "esports broadcast stage", because Xross Stars is a streamer card game), carried through every surface: the title (CSS wordmark, crossing stage beams, a scrolling perspective floor), the HUD as a broadcast scoreboard, the event feed as a lower-third caption bar, a "REC" ticker, neon card frames, its own card backs, type, motion, music and an original announcer. **How to apply:** at kickoff write a one-line presentation concept tied to the game's identity, plus a "must not look like" list naming the template and the last two siblings, and get the owner's yes on it with the art audition. Keep the template's layout rules and modules (rule 16: keep the structure), but rewrite every visual token: palette, typefaces, frame shapes and ornaments, card backs, zone shapes, title and menu screens, transitions and stingers, icons, music and voice. Before the first playable ships, put screenshots of the title, the deck picker and a mid-game board beside the template's and the previous game's; if one could be mistaken for the other, it is not done. Re-colouring is not re-skinning.
22. *(added 2026-11-02, MANAFORGE, owner)* **Every game saves the game in progress, and the player can come back to the same spot.** Owner, 2026-11-02, after MANAFORGE got it (its PLAN D26): "update our knowledge base GitHub so that al[l] future game[s] contain a save spot so they can come back to the same spot in the future". Two shipped versions to copy from: Mallet-42k M10-M12 ([mallet-42k.md](mallet-42k.md)) and MANAFORGE (`js/ui.js` `saveGame`, `js/main.js` `readSave`, 2026-11-02; on the unmerged branch `claude/izzet-prowess-orzhov-demons-a07rsk`, not on `main` as of 2026-11-03; [manaforge.md](manaforge.md)). Build it with the first playable ([recipe.md](recipe.md) step 5), not at polish: Legion Forge only added it at M7. **What every game must do:**
    - **Autosave after every accepted action and when a game starts**, so closing the tab at any moment loses nothing. A save button may be added, but it is never the only way.
    - **One versioned envelope per save:** the setup (sides, decks or factions, seats, difficulty, options), the seed, the action list, and a snapshot of the state. Add whatever the interface needs to reopen honestly, such as the recently-played strip, but not the presentation cursor.
    - **The snapshot is what reopens**, so it is the same spot even after a rules update. The action list rides along so the bug report still replays from the seed. Mallet instead reopens by replay and discards a save that no longer replays to the same position, so a rules fix can cost the player the game they were in. Prefer the snapshot, which needs the state to be plain JSON from M2 on: no class instances, functions or Maps. MANAFORGE measured 40 KB (a duel, turn 13) to 93 KB (a cEDH game, turn 19), reopening in about 1 ms against 0.7 s to replay 604 actions.
    - **The start screen offers Continue, naming what it reopens:** both sides, the turn or round, whose turn it is, life or score, and when it was saved. It reopens on the current board without replaying animation, and the AI carries on if it was the AI's turn.
    - **Discard asks once more.** "New game" says it replaces the save, there is one slot unless the game needs more, and a finished game clears its save.
    - **Never silent:** a save that can't be opened says why (unreadable, content no longer in the game, a state the engine can't read) and offers to discard it. A browser that refuses to store shows a line in the game's UI. Rule 8 and the no-silent-fallback rule both apply.
    - **Storage key per game:** every Pages game shares the `dragoonant.github.io` origin, and so shares one `localStorage`, so a bare `save` key would collide across games; MANAFORGE uses `mf-save`. `file://`, a local server and Pages are separate origins with separate saves, and "How to play" says so.
    - **Verified in a real browser with a persistent profile** (Playwright `launchPersistentContext`): play a few turns, close the browser, reopen, Continue, and compare turn, step, log length, hands and action count. Then play on and check that the bug report still replays to the live state. Also cover Discard, a corrupt save, missing content, a finished game and refused storage. In the headless sim, a save/load round trip is an invariant (Dead of Winter did this).
23. *(added 2026-11-03; owner's standing instruction of 2026-10-31, Mallet 42k `docs/FIGURE-STYLE.md`)* **Never use these words in any image-generation prompt, edit request, prompt template, prompt table or agent brief:** chibi, MGSD, SD, super-deformed, Master Grade SD, Gundam, Nendoroid, cute, kawaii, toy, cartoony, stubby, tiny legs. The owner has asked for this repeatedly. The labels pushed the image model to big-eyed, baby-faced figures that the owner rejected. Describe figures in plain measurements and materials instead (head-heights, head share of height, shoulder width, leg length, finish) and follow rule 7. **This list is the only place in the KB where these words appear**, so KB text copied into a brief cannot carry one over; where an older project's prompt used them, its entry paraphrases. The narrower bans earlier projects kept ("mecha kit", model-kit brand words: Whirr Machine, Fell-in, Dead of Winner, Murder Squad) still apply.
24. *(added 2026-11-04, assembly-forge, owner)* **A long press on a phone never selects text, pops a callout or flashes a highlight.** Owner, 2026-11-04: "make sure holding down on mobile form factors doesn't select text - it always highlights shit in the game, super annoying. Update our knowledge base too so all of our games never suffer from that again." Every game ships this in its base stylesheet from the first playable, and keeps text selectable only where someone types:
    ```css
    html, body, #root { -webkit-user-select: none; user-select: none; -webkit-touch-callout: none; -webkit-tap-highlight-color: transparent; }
    input, textarea, select, [contenteditable='true'] { -webkit-user-select: text; user-select: text; -webkit-touch-callout: default; }
    img, canvas { -webkit-user-drag: none; }
    button { touch-action: manipulation; }
    ```
    plus, as a fallback for browsers that ignore the CSS, `selectstart` and `contextmenu` listeners on `document` that call `preventDefault()` unless the target is an input, textarea, select or editable element. Gate it with an e2e check at phone size: `getComputedStyle(document.body).userSelect === 'none'`, and a triple click on HUD text leaves `getSelection()` empty (assembly-forge `tests/e2e/smoke.spec.ts`, "the HUD fits a phone in landscape").
25. *(added 2026-11-04, assembly-forge, owner)* **Units are picked by their tile, never by their model, and a model never blocks a tap on the tile behind it.** Owner, 2026-11-04: "the picking of a unit is really hard in the game, I think because we allow the player to tap on the model of the unit to pick it. It should only be where the player can only select units by clicking on its tile. The unit models should never occlude the touch of the player from selecting a tile behind the unit." Applies to every board game with figures on a grid (hex or square), and was asked for by name for three-kingdoms-tactics. **How to apply:**
    - Pointer handlers live on the tiles only (one instanced mesh, or one invisible pick plane per grid). Figures, props, health bars, rings and effects carry **no** `onClick`/`onPointer*` handlers and no invisible hit capsules. In React Three Fiber an object only takes part in picking when it has a handler, so removing the handlers is enough; in plain three.js, raycast against the tile set only (or set `mesh.raycast = () => {}` on figures).
    - A tap on a hex resolves in one place: the legal action for that hex first (move there, deploy, attack whatever stands there, play an armed card on it), otherwise select the unit standing on it, otherwise clear the selection.
    - Hovering a hex shows the card of the unit on it, so the hover info a model used to give is not lost.
    - The proof is an e2e test that taps the screen point of a hex drawn behind a standing figure and expects that hex's action to happen (assembly-forge, "a tap on the hex behind a machine reaches that hex").
    Assembly Forge had both a per-figure click handler and an invisible capsule the size of the machine "so a click lands on it rather than the hex behind"; that capsule is exactly what made picking hard.

# The owner's standing rules

Earned across more than 30 projects; apply them without re-asking. *(2026-11-03)* Moved out of `README.md` with their numbering unchanged, so "README rule N" in an older doc or game repo means rule N here.

1. **Playable first.** Something the owner can open on Pages and play beats test coverage. Every stage ends in something clickable, with a screenshot.
2. **Fidelity is the product.** Mechanics ported 1:1 from the current edition; never tune printed numbers; log every ambiguity as `RULING: <rule> | <what we did> | <why>` with a status word instead of blocking.
3. **"Mallet posture" on IP.** Real unit/weapon/ability/team names are fine; all prose (rules text, lore, UI copy) in our own words; no publisher art, icons or logos; official PDFs and community data stay in gitignored `docs/sources/`; README and UI say "unofficial, non-commercial fan project". GW iconography in generated figures is an accepted playtest placeholder.
4. **Do the legwork; research before assuming.** Free downloads, $0 checkouts, Vassal modules, fan wikis via curl, product photos: all fair game. Only stop for passwords, payment or account creation. Never ship a stand-in or "unverified" value after one blocked source.
5. **Answer before building.** When the owner asks questions, answer and propose, then wait for the go. Explicit instructions mean act.
6. **Cost-sensitive.** Sonnet implements, the session model integrates and verifies only where rules are dense; one or two agents per milestone with exact file lists; no adversarial review loops by default; about 2.5M tokens per push; check-ins of 10 lines or fewer. Mallet's big-spec experiment (4.8M before anything playable) is not to be repeated.
7. **Art direction is set once per project and not re-litigated.** *(rewritten 2026-11-03)* For miniature figures the house style is Mallet 42k `docs/FIGURE-STYLE.md`, approved by the owner on 2026-10-31 ("Lock it in. Those are way better."): serious and highly detailed like a premium painted miniature, about 2.8 head-heights with the head or helmet about 35% of the height, very broad armoured shoulders about 1.8 times the head's width, short thick legs about one head long, a covered or stern face, and matte realistic paint with edge highlights. Attach `figure-style-reference.jpg` to the first prompt of each army chat and describe the figure in measurements and materials only (rule 23). The five locked test figures are in Mallet 42k `art/unit-concepts/style-tests/`. This replaced the earlier large-head style (about 2-2.5 heads, head about 40% of height, rounded shapes, glossy paint), whose labels pushed the image model to big-eyed, baby-faced figures. Check the image, not the prompt. Concept approval is delegated to Claude; send contact sheets as FYI. Lock the figure look early (a late lock threw away a whole v1 set).
8. **No native tooltips in games**, prompts name what they decide, open information is a rule, never assume a choice, every displayed number comes from the engine.
9. **Audio is judged by ear on `sounds.html`**; say "not verified by ear" until the owner listens. The synth score was rejected once ("the music sucks"), so real generated music is expected. Since 2026-10-17 the local Audio Studio replaces ElevenLabs.
10. **GPU etiquette:** announce GPU work; never two GPU jobs at once; never kill node by image name. The owner waived the ask-first gate on 2026-10-17 for Fell-in and said to use the studios to develop the look and sound.
11. **Privacy:** credit "evilgouki"; never the real name or home path in anything that can become public; grep before every commit.
12. Questions go through the multiple-choice UI, batched (two batches of at most 4), recommended option first, everything else stated as a default.
13. **Pipeline everything; never idle a stage.** Each finished item moves to the next stage straight away: a concept PNG goes into the GLB queue the moment it renders, and a GLB is finalised and wired the moment it lands. Both GPUs (this PC and Alteisen) stay busy while concepts are still coming. The same goes for code: a team's verification starts when its implementer finishes, not when the whole batch does. Waiting for a full batch before starting the next stage was called out as inefficient (Murder Squad, 2026-10-18). **Quality gates also run per item, inside the stream.** Each concept passes a measurable check before it is kept: *(updated 2026-11-03)* a side-by-side with `figure-style-reference.jpg` or the locked test figures, a head of about 35% of figure height and about 2.8 head-heights overall, broad shoulders, and a stern or covered face (rule 7). The coordinator reviews the first image and the contact sheet of each team before that team's GLBs start. "Check each image" with no concrete reference let 32 off-model concepts through, and the owner caught it, not us (2026-10-24).

22. *(added 2026-11-02, MANAFORGE, owner)* **Every game saves the game in progress, and the player can come back to the same spot.** Owner, 2026-11-02, after MANAFORGE got it (its PLAN D26): "update our knowledge base GitHub so that al[l] future game[s] contain a save spot so they can come back to the same spot in the future". Two shipped versions to copy from: Mallet-42k M10-M12 ([mallet-42k.md](mallet-42k.md)) and MANAFORGE (`js/ui.js` `saveGame`, `js/main.js` `readSave`, 2026-11-02; on the unmerged branch `claude/izzet-prowess-orzhov-demons-a07rsk`, not on `main` as of 2026-11-03; [manaforge.md](manaforge.md)). Build it with the first playable ([recipe.md](recipe.md) step 5), not at polish: Legion Forge only added it at M7. **What every game must do:**
    - **Autosave after every accepted action and when a game starts**, so closing the tab at any moment loses nothing. A save button may be added, but it is never the only way.
    - **One versioned envelope per save:** the setup (sides, decks or factions, seats, difficulty, options), the seed, the action list, and a snapshot of the state. Add whatever the interface needs to reopen honestly, such as the recently-played strip, but not the presentation cursor.
    - **The snapshot is what reopens**, so it is the same spot even after a rules update. The action list rides along so the bug report still replays from the seed. Mallet instead reopens by replay and discards a save that no longer replays to the same position, so a rules fix can cost the player the game they were in. Prefer the snapshot, which needs the state to be plain JSON from M2 on: no class instances, functions or Maps. MANAFORGE measured 40 KB (a duel, turn 13) to 93 KB (a cEDH game, turn 19), reopening in about 1 ms against 0.7 s to replay 604 actions.
    - **The start screen offers Continue, naming what it reopens:** both sides, the turn or round, whose turn it is, life or score, and when it was saved. It reopens on the current board without replaying animation, and the AI carries on if it was the AI's turn.
    - **Discard asks once more.** "New game" says it replaces the save, there is one slot unless the game needs more, and a finished game clears its save.
    - **Never silent:** a save that can't be opened says why (unreadable, content no longer in the game, a state the engine can't read) and offers to discard it. A browser that refuses to store shows a line in the game's UI. Rule 8 and the no-silent-fallback rule both apply.
    - **Storage key per game:** every Pages game shares the `dragoonant.github.io` origin, and so shares one `localStorage`, so a bare `save` key would collide across games; MANAFORGE uses `mf-save`. `file://`, a local server and Pages are separate origins with separate saves, and "How to play" says so.
    - **Verified in a real browser with a persistent profile** (Playwright `launchPersistentContext`): play a few turns, close the browser, reopen, Continue, and compare turn, step, log length, hands and action count. Then play on and check that the bug report still replays to the live state. Also cover Discard, a corrupt save, missing content, a finished game and refused storage. In the headless sim, a save/load round trip is an invariant (Dead of Winter did this).
23. *(added 2026-11-03; owner's standing instruction of 2026-10-31, Mallet 42k `docs/FIGURE-STYLE.md`)* **Never use these words in any image-generation prompt, edit request, prompt template, prompt table or agent brief:** chibi, MGSD, SD, super-deformed, Master Grade SD, Gundam, Nendoroid, cute, kawaii, toy, cartoony, stubby, tiny legs. The owner has asked for this repeatedly. The labels pushed the image model to big-eyed, baby-faced figures that the owner rejected. Describe figures in plain measurements and materials instead (head-heights, head share of height, shoulder width, leg length, finish) and follow rule 7. **This list is the only place in the KB where these words appear**, so KB text copied into a brief cannot carry one over; where an older project's prompt used them, its entry paraphrases. 24. *(added 2026-11-04, assembly-forge, owner)* **A long press on a phone never selects text, pops a callout or flashes a highlight.** Owner, 2026-11-04: "make sure holding down on mobile form factors doesn't select text - it always highlights shit in the game, super annoying. Update our knowledge base too so all of our games never suffer from that again." Every game ships this in its base stylesheet from the first playable, and keeps text selectable only where someone types:
    ```css
    html, body, #root { -webkit-user-select: none; user-select: none; -webkit-touch-callout: none; -webkit-tap-highlight-color: transparent; }
    input, textarea, select, [contenteditable='true'] { -webkit-user-select: text; user-select: text; -webkit-touch-callout: default; }
    img, canvas { -webkit-user-drag: none; }
    button { touch-action: manipulation; }
    ```
    plus, as a fallback for browsers that ignore the CSS, `selectstart` and `contextmenu` listeners on `document` that call `preventDefault()` unless the target is an input, textarea, select or editable element. Gate it with an e2e check at phone size: `getComputedStyle(document.body).userSelect === 'none'`, and a triple click on HUD text leaves `getSelection()` empty (assembly-forge `tests/e2e/smoke.spec.ts`, "the HUD fits a phone in landscape").
25. *(added 2026-11-04, assembly-forge, owner)* **Units are picked by their tile, never by their model, and a model never blocks a tap on the tile behind it.** Owner, 2026-11-04: "the picking of a unit is really hard in the game, I think because we allow the player to tap on the model of the unit to pick it. It should only be where the player can only select units by clicking on its tile. The unit models should never occlude the touch of the player from selecting a tile behind the unit." Applies to every board game with figures on a grid (hex or square), and was asked for by name for three-kingdoms-tactics. **How to apply:**
    - Pointer handlers live on the tiles only (one instanced mesh, or one invisible pick plane per grid). Figures, props, health bars, rings and effects carry **no** `onClick`/`onPointer*` handlers and no invisible hit capsules. In React Three Fiber an object only takes part in picking when it has a handler, so removing the handlers is enough; in plain three.js, raycast against the tile set only (or set `mesh.raycast = () => {}` on figures).
    - A tap on a hex resolves in one place: the legal action for that hex first (move there, deploy, attack whatever stands there, play an armed card on it), otherwise select the unit standing on it, otherwise clear the selection.
    - Hovering a hex shows the card of the unit on it, so the hover info a model used to give is not lost.
    - The proof is an e2e test that taps the screen point of a hex drawn behind a standing figure and expects that hex's action to happen (assembly-forge, "a tap on the hex behind a machine reaches that hex").
    Assembly Forge had both a per-figure click handler and an invisible capsule the size of the machine "so a click lands on it rather than the hex behind"; that capsule is exactly what made picking hard.
26. *(added 2026-11-04, assembly-forge, owner)* **A model is loaded before it is shown: never draw the stand-in or placeholder in its place while it loads.** Owner, 2026-11-04: "i don't like seeing the procedural model in place of the real model while deploying models ... Always load the model before showing the model on screen, so we don't see the procedural model." **How to apply:**
    - Start every GLB downloading when the app opens (drei `useGLTF.preload(url)` for each enabled model, called once at startup), not when the first unit using it appears.
    - The `<Suspense>` fallback around a model is `null` (draw nothing for that frame), never the stand-in. A procedural stand-in or primitive is only the error fallback, for a model that failed to load.
    - The button that starts a match waits for the loads (drei `useProgress`: `!active && loaded >= total`) and says so ("Loading models 5/13"), so a game never opens with models still arriving.
    - Assembly Forge: `src/client/board/preload.ts`, `Figure.tsx`, `Prop.tsx`, the briefing's Deploy button.
27. *(added 2026-11-04, assembly-forge, owner)* **Mouse controls on every 3D board:** the wheel zooms (towards the cursor), the right button drag rotates (and tilts), the middle button drag pans, and the left button picks. "unless it's a 2 button mouse, then it will also be panning the screen." A browser cannot ask how many buttons a mouse has, so: the left drag pans until the first middle-button press shows the mouse has one; from then on the left button only picks, remembered per browser (`localStorage`, a per-game key). `preventDefault()` on a middle `pointerdown`, or Windows browsers start autoscroll. Touch is unchanged: one finger pans, two twist and pinch. Gate: an e2e test that a left drag pans on a fresh profile, a middle drag pans, a left drag after that leaves the camera alone, and a right drag rotates (assembly-forge `src/client/board/mapController.ts`, `tests/e2e/smoke.spec.ts` "mouse:").
28. *(added 2026-11-28, magitek-wars, owner)* **The game fills the window, at any size and any shape.** The owner has asked for this on several games. In Magitek Wars, of the 512 x 448 frame shown at 1x in a 1280 x 720 window: "Why is the screen so small?  This looks so dumb" (PLAN D21), then, of the square frame: "why is the screen a square?  Can you just use the rest of teh screen space?" (D23). [card-games.md](card-games.md) section 5 already said "the board is the whole window", but only as a lesson. **How to apply:**
    - Never hold the frame to whole-number scales, and never letterbox it to a fixed aspect. Fix the logical height, let the logical width follow the window's shape between a minimum and a maximum (Magitek Wars: height 448, width 512 to 1280), then scale to the largest size that fits.
    - Give the canvas one backing pixel per device pixel (`devicePixelRatio`), and redo the fit on `resize`. If any art is pixel-styled, draw an in-between scale sharp at the next whole number and reduce it smoothly ("sharp bilinear"), so edges stay crisp and pixel columns stay even.
    - The play screen spreads across the width: Line slots spread evenly, wider hand cards show the whole art, the log gets wider, and the battlefield covers the background. A screen built for a narrow width (title, menus, deck builder, map) sits in the middle with a soft blurred, darkened copy of itself at the sides, never black bars.
    - DOM games: the same result with `100dvw`/`100dvh` layouts and no fixed-pixel stage.
    - Gate: screenshots at 1280 x 720, 1920 x 1080, 2560 x 1080 and a phone held both ways, checked for no bars on the play screen and no scrollbars.
    - Magitek Wars: `src/main.ts` `fit()`, `present()` and `compose()` (commits `1964f4e`, `9b0c28c`).
29. *(added 2026-11-28, magitek-wars, owner)* **The interface never looks 8-bit or 16-bit, even when the art is pixel-styled.** In Magitek Wars the owner said it twice. First: "The title screen is looks too 8-bit, make it look a little nicer" (D13). Then, after the code-drawn interface kept a 5 x 7 bitmap font and pixel windows: "The game screens look too 8-bit, let's do an overhaul. Let's move away from that look, it's ruining the look of the game. Base it more on the new art style that we've trained, not pixelated or 8/16-bit. The art style we've trained carries a lot of the look I want." (D25). **How to apply:**
    - Everything drawn in code is drawn smoothly at the screen's own resolution: real typefaces bundled with the game (Magitek Wars: Cinzel Bold for headings and buttons, Fira Sans for everything read), anti-aliased panels, rounded frames, soft shadows, and glow highlights instead of a pixel cursor. Transparency washes replace dithers.
    - Prose is in sentence case; only headings and buttons are capitals. Pick a text face whose digits read clearly on canvas: Alegreya Sans was dropped because canvas cannot switch off its old-style figures, so its 0 read as o.
    - The game's art sets the look of the interface, not the other way round.
    - If the art has both a pixel version and a full-detail version, full detail is the default, and the other version is a switch. The owner asked "How about the pixelating thing? Is it a filter you can turn off?", then "Full detail, with toggle" (D22). The switch sits on the title menu and is kept per browser.
    - Magitek Wars: `src/ui.ts` (the kit), `src/font.ts`, `docs/ART-STYLE.md` section 7 (commits `e1b01f2`, `d04694d`, `b13255b`).
30. *(added 2026-11-28, magitek-wars, owner)* **The layout of a card battle screen.** Owner, by voice, 2026-11-27: "I want to be able to see ... my cards at the bottom of the screen in the center instead of left justified. I also want to see the opponent's cards at the top of the screen. Doesn't have to take up a lot of space, just enough so that I can see how many cards visually that they have ... I also want you to display the draw and discard pile, as well as ... whatever other decks are in the game. So that I can click on them and see what cards I have left in my draw pile, as well as whatever has been played in the discard pile um, and the exile pile ..." Also: "the log in the middle screen just takes up too much space. We had a log drawer ... So let's make a drawer". And: "I think in Annals of ... Scriven Earth, we had um, it showed ... which ... characters were fighting, and blocking just by moving the cards forward." Every card game ships this layout from its first playable:
    - **Your hand is centred at the bottom**, between your draw pile on the left and your discard pile on the right. A big hand overlaps, and the hovered card lifts.
    - **The opponent's hand is shown as card backs along the top**, small but countable at a glance, with their piles beside it.
    - **Every pile in the game is drawn and clickable**: draw pile, discard, exile, and whatever else the rules have, on both sides, each with its count. Your draw pile opens to show the cards left in it, sorted so the draw order stays hidden. Discard and exile piles show their cards in the order they were played. The opponent's draw pile shows a count only. Viewers show cards, not text lists (rule 33).
    - **The log is a drawer** that slides over the board (a button plus a key, scrolled with the mouse wheel). It never sits in the middle of the board, and opening it never moves the board.
    - **Combat is shown on the board**: attackers step forward out of their row, and each blocker moves across to face the attacker it blocks. Lines run from each attacker to its blocker, or to the player it hits when unblocked.
    - **Life and resources can be read at a glance, for both sides.** The owner pointed to Legends of Runeterra and Hearthstone as the bar: "Super simple, very intuitive, very good looking, and you can quickly tell how much life you and your opponent have, and you can easily see how much charge/reserve you have" (2026-11-27). Asked to pick one of five drawn layouts, the owner chose a dial per player (Magitek Wars "Clockface": life at the hub, resources as rings around it). Holding or dragging a card pulses the resources it would spend.
    - Magitek Wars: `src/match/board.ts` (`drawHand`, `drawFoeHand`, `drawPiles`, `drawPanel`, `drawDrawer`, `columns`, `drawCombat`; commit `0525940`). The Clockface screen is PLAN D37 (commit `3e8365c`, merged to `main` in `7da406c` on 2026-11-28).
31. *(added 2026-11-28, magitek-wars, owner)* **Cards are dragged onto the board to play them, into any legal open spot.** Owner, 2026-11-27: "I'd like to be able to drag cards onto the board to play them. We should be able to drag a unit card onto any legal open spot on the board, not just fill in the spaces left to right." **How to apply:**
    - A press that moves past a threshold becomes a drag (Magitek Wars: 6 px for a mouse, 10 px for a finger; distance, never time). Click to arm a card, then click its target, still works.
    - A unit takes the open slot it is dropped on, or the nearest open one. A spell or tactic is dropped on its target; with no target, a drop on the board plays it; with several targets left, the prompt asks for the rest. Dropping it back over the hand cancels.
    - On a full row, dropping on one of your own units chooses that unit to be replaced, and the replacement waits for a confirm button that names it (SCRAP AND PLAY, rule 33).
    - A card that cannot be played refuses the drag and shows the engine's reason (`whyNot`). The drag path and the click path are both filters over `legalActions`, so they cannot disagree.
    - Where a slot has no rules meaning, the chosen place is display-only and kept per match in storage. If any card reads position (buff your neighbours), the position is engine state.
    - Magitek Wars: `src/match/board.ts` `startDrag`, `dropCol`, `drop`, `drawDragged` (commit `b72a82f`).
32. *(added 2026-11-28, magitek-wars, owner)* **Inspecting a card enlarges it where it sits.** It never opens in a fixed side panel. Owner, 2026-11-27: "when holding down on cards to see the full card and its text, enlarge the card being played where it is instead of placing it on the right side of the screen every time. The enlarged card should go away after the player lifts their finger. Same with mouse - enlarge the card on mouse over, shrink it back to original size when moving the cursor away from the card." **How to apply:**
    - Mouse: the full card (art, name, type, text, keyword help, live stats, damage, level progress) grows out of the card under the pointer, and shrinks away when the pointer leaves.
    - Touch: a press held for 400 ms inspects instead of acting, and the card shrinks away when the finger lifts. A tap acts on release, not on press, so a hold or a drag never fires a click.
    - The enlarged card is kept on screen; hand cards grow upward. It works the same for hand cards, units, the stack, heroes, and cards in every viewer.
    - **Every card the player can see, on every screen, can be opened large**, not only on the battle screen. The owner had to ask again on 2026-11-28 (PLAN D36): "When picking cards through the expedition, I'm not able to zoom in on the cards to see the card details. Fix that. I should be able to zoom in on cards wherever I can see them." That covers rewards, shop cards, recruits, hero pictures (both faces), a foe's deck in a briefing and every deck grid. On those screens Magitek Wars adds a small lens button on each card, and a right-click or a 400 ms hold works too. Opening a card never picks it (`lens` and `zoomCard` in `src/cardgrid.ts`).
    - Rule 24 stops the long press from selecting text.
    - Magitek Wars: `src/match/board.ts` `pointerDown`, `pointerUp`, `drawDetail`; `src/main.ts` touch hold (commits `d484bc8`, `b72a82f`).
33. *(added 2026-11-28, magitek-wars, owner)* **Every time the player picks a card, they see the cards and confirm the pick.** Owner, 2026-11-27: "When removing a card, I'm given a list of text.  Yuck.  Change that so I can see the cards, and let em confirm that the card I pick is the right one to remove.  Do this for all other mechanics that require me to pick a card." **How to apply:**

34. *(added 2026-11-28, magitek-wars, owner)* **Every game event has a visible effect, and the effects are driven by the log.** Owner, 2026-11-27: "Eventually I do want to see special effects all throughout the game for attacks, spells, etc. start with some simple effects for now, but eventually I want it to be exciting when cards are played and attacks are made." [card-games.md](card-games.md) section 5 says the animation layer was deferred by five or six projects running; build it with the first playable. **How to apply:**
    - The engine's log is the only trigger. Each new entry becomes an effect, so an effect can never show something the engine did not do. In Magitek Wars:
      - a played card is named in a banner with its art;
      - units fly in from the hand;
      - strikes lunge and slash;
      - damage sparks, shakes and shows a floating number;
      - kills shatter;
      - spells fly as bolts from the caster's side to their targets;
